# Sunnystep Blog Style Guide

## Grammar & Spelling
- **Locale**: Singapore English (British spelling)
  - colour, favour, minimise, organise, recognise, practise (verb), practice (noun)
- **Numbers**: Spell out one to nine; numerals for 10 and above
- **Percentages**: Always numerals — 5%, not "five percent"
- **Currency**: SGD or S$ — never just $ (ambiguous in Singapore)
- **Oxford comma**: Yes — "comfort, style, and science"

## Sentences & Paragraphs
- Mix short punchy sentences with longer explanatory ones
- Active voice preferred — "Sunnystep engineered the insole" not "The insole was engineered"
- Paragraphs: 2–4 sentences. One idea per paragraph. Plenty of white space.
- Average sentence length: 15–20 words

## Headlines & Subheadings
- **H1**: Clear, benefit-driven, includes primary keyword
- **H2s**: Informative and scannable — reader should understand the article from H2s alone
- **Capitalisation**: Sentence case — "How to choose comfortable office shoes" not "How To Choose Comfortable Office Shoes"
- Use numbers when helpful: "5 Signs Your Shoes Are Hurting Your Feet"

## Formatting
- **Bold**: Key terms or important takeaways — use sparingly
- **Italics**: Light emphasis or product names
- **Lists**: Use for 3+ items; keep list items parallel in structure
- **Tables**: Good for comparisons (shoe features, product specs)

## CTAs (Calls to Action)
- At least one within the article body, one at the end
- Tone: Direct but not pushy
- ✅ "Explore our range of comfortable flats for women →"
- ✅ "See why 5,000+ customers have switched to Sunnystep"
- ❌ "BUY NOW!!!" / "Click here to purchase"

## Terminology

| Say This | Not That | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Comfortable shoes | Comfy shoes | Too casual |
| Arch support | Arch cushioning | Less precise |
| Wide toe box | Wide fit | Less descriptive |
| Acupressure insole | Massage insole | Sounds gimmicky |
| Science-backed | Scientifically proven | Overused claim |
| Physiotherapist | Physio | Spell out on first use |
| SGD / S$ | $ | Ambiguous |

## What to Avoid
- Superlatives without proof: "the most comfortable shoe in the world"
- Generic filler openers: "In today's fast-paced world…", "Are you looking for…?"
- AI writing clichés: "delve into", "it's worth noting", "in conclusion", "tapestry"
- Excessive exclamation marks — maximum one per article
- Passive voice as the default
- Mentioning competitor brand names negatively — differentiate factually, not disparagingly
