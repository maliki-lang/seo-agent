# Sunnystep Brand Voice

## Who We Are

Sunnystep is a science-led footwear brand that brings joy to people on the move. We combine science, sustainability, and style to minimise stress on your body and support every move — from heel to toe.

**Mission:** Empower people to move freely and happily.
**Tagline:** Life is Suntastic
**Brand Pillars:** Science | Happiness | Movement
**Market:** Singapore (primary), Malaysia

## Origin Story

Founded by Mao Ting after a postpartum spine injury that made everyday movement painful. She couldn't find shoes that combined real comfort with style, so she built Sunnystep. The brand was built during a dark period — the name reflects what she wanted it to stand for: sunny, cheerful, uplifting.

## Brand Archetype

**Hero** — We empower people, ignite their determination, and encourage them to fuel their own hero's journey. Customers feel inner strength and belief in their ability to overcome challenges.

## Voice Pillars

### 1. Empowering
- Use action-oriented language. Affirm the audience's strengths.
- **Example**: "Your moves inspire us. You're unstoppable."
- **Avoid**: Passive, tentative language ("you might want to consider…")

### 2. Positive
- Maintain an uplifting tone. Highlight benefits. Focus on what's gained, not what's lost.
- **Example**: "Here's how we can help you move better every day."
- **Avoid**: Negativity, doom-and-gloom framing, clinical coldness

### 3. Visionary
- Paint a picture of transformation. Look forward, not backward.
- **Example**: "Imagine ending the day without the usual foot ache."
- **Avoid**: Dwelling too long on pain without pivoting to the solution

### 4. Direct and Warm
- Speak plainly about pain points — don't dress them up in corporate speak.
- Warm but not fluffy. Empathetic, not saccharine.
- **Example**: "Most office shoes were not designed for 10-hour days. Ours were."
- **Avoid**: Hollow marketing language ("best-in-class", "revolutionary")

## Tone Guidelines

Write like a knowledgeable friend who genuinely wants you to feel better. You know the science, but you explain it simply. You're confident, not boastful — the product proof and customer stories do the heavy lifting.

**Singapore-aware**: References to CBD commutes, long standing shifts, humid weather, and hectic work days resonate locally. Use Singapore English spelling (e.g. "colour", "favour", "minimise").

**Science is a reason to trust, not a lecture.** Reference MIT Sports Lab, physiotherapist recommendations, and acupressure technology as credibility signals — never as a lecture.

## Tone by Content Type

**How-To / Educational**: Clear, practical, step-by-step. Authoritative but approachable.
**Product-Focused**: Lead with the benefit. Feature → reason to believe → outcome.
**Lifestyle**: Warm, aspirational, relatable. Real people, real settings.
**Pain Point**: Direct and empathetic. Name the problem clearly, then pivot to the solution quickly.

## Editorial Thesis Requirement

**Every article must argue something.** Not just "here is information about topic X" — but a specific claim that could be disagreed with.

Before writing, answer: *What is the one thing this article argues that competitors don't say?*

Good thesis examples from Sunnystep's published articles:
- "Memory foam, despite its reputation, is actually bad for your feet if you're walking all day — here's the physics of why."
- "We made our shoes from used coffee grounds, and here's exactly how the process works."
- "Comfort in footwear is the result of specific structural features, not a vague feeling — and most marketed 'comfort' features don't deliver."

Bad (no thesis): "Here's what to look for in comfortable shoes in Singapore."
Good (has thesis): "Most shoes marketed as comfortable fail Singapore conditions — here's why, and what actually works."

The thesis shapes everything: the hook, the structure, what gets included, how the brand is introduced. Without a thesis, the article is just covering a topic. With one, it's making a case.

## What NOT to Sound Like

❌ "Utilising cutting-edge biomechanical innovation, our patented insole technology delivers superior plantar fascia support."
✅ "The insoles are designed with your arch in mind — so your feet don't scream at you by 3pm."

❌ "We are pleased to offer a range of stylish footwear options for the modern consumer."
✅ "One pair. Office to dinner. No costume change needed."

## Audience

**Primary**: Women aged 28–50, urban Singapore professionals and frequent walkers. On their feet a lot — whether at a desk job, retail, teaching, or travelling. Style-conscious but increasingly prioritising comfort. May have experienced foot, knee, or back pain.

**Secondary**: Men seeking everyday comfort; parents buying for kids (separate USP set applies).

**What they care about**: Not having to choose between looking good and feeling good. Getting through a long day without foot pain. Shoes that work across occasions (office, weekend, travel).
