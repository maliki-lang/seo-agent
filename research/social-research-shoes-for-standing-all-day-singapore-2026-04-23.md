# Social Research: Shoes for Standing All Day Singapore

**Date**: 2026-04-23

---

## Key Insights from Expert Sources & Community Forums

### Cleveland Clinic / Dr. Stephen Smik (podiatric physician)
- **URL**: https://consultqd.clevelandclinic.org/nursing-self-care-keep-feet-feeling-their-best-before-during-and-after-shifts
- Nurses take at least **9,000 steps per shift** — but the bigger problem is sustained static standing, not walking
- Dr. Smik: **"Running shoes are the way to go"** for 8-12 hour shifts — specifically recommends AGAINST foam clog-type shoes because they "lack heel counter and arch support" (counter-intuitive since Dansko clogs are frequently recommended elsewhere)
- **"Nurses need a firmness and stiffness to the arch, so a more rigid material like thermoplastic is ideal"** — not memory foam
- Good fit: shoe "hugs the foot (specifically the heel) comfortably, but allows the toes to wiggle freely"

### Kuru Footwear / Nurse Research
- **URL**: https://www.kurufootwear.com/pages/shoes-work-best-shoes-for-nurses-guide
- Nurses walk **4–5 miles during a single 12-hour shift**
- **Shoe support diminishes after 6 hours** — yet shifts are 8-12 hours. What are nurses standing on for the last 2-6 hours?
- Nursing carries **higher injury risk than construction or law enforcement** — rarely mentioned outside healthcare circles
- ~35,000 back injuries annually among nurses — foot issues are the upstream cause of much of this

### Michigan Avenue Podiatry
- **URL**: https://michiganavenuepodiatry.com/pain-from-standing-on-feet-all-day
- Morning stiffness and sharp discomfort upon standing described as signature plantar fasciitis pattern
- "Pain that doesn't simply fade when you clock out" — key phrase capturing what standing workers actually experience
- Even 30 seconds of walking or stretching every hour helps — which means the static nature of standing is the core problem, not movement

### Hurst Podiatry (on teachers, nurses, retail workers)
- **URL**: https://www.hurstpodiatry.com.au/blog/common-foot-issues-teachers-nurses-retail-workers/
- Workers struggle finding "supportive shoes that don't scream retirement home chic" — the style-function tension is real
- Conditions that develop: plantar fasciitis, bunions, heel pain, tingling and numbness from compressed nerves

---

## Pain Points Identified

1. **The 6-hour collapse**: shoe cushioning compresses fully after 6 hours and doesn't recover — most standing workers don't know this is happening
2. **The memory foam trap**: sold as premium comfort, actively bad for standing workers because it compresses under sustained load and never recovers
3. **The professional appearance constraint**: nurses, teachers, retail workers can't just wear running shoes — dress codes limit the obvious fix
4. **Morning heel pain**: plantar fasciitis manifests most painfully in the first steps of the morning — the shift's damage shows up before the next shift starts
5. **Buying new shoes every few months**: workers cycle through "comfort" shoes that fail, spending money without solving the problem
6. **Singapore heat + hard floors**: swelling accelerates in heat, and Singapore's marble/tile/concrete surfaces are harder than most Western equivalents

## Real Language to Use

- "pain that doesn't simply fade when you clock out"
- "the first steps in the morning" — plantar fasciitis signature
- "by midday" — when cushioning has collapsed
- "can't just wear running shoes" — the professional constraint
- "12-hour shift" / "8-hour shift" — the real measurement unit, not "all day"
- "my feet are done" — common end-of-shift language
- "supportive shoes that don't scream retirement home chic" — the style-function tension

## Language to Avoid

- "ergonomic footwear"
- "advanced cushioning technology"
- "all-day comfort"
- "supportive design"

## Surprising / Counterintuitive Angles

1. **Nurses walk 4-5 miles per shift, but standing is the bigger problem than walking** — the dynamic load of walking is actually easier on the foot than static compression
2. **Memory foam is bad for standing workers** — the "premium" comfort feature that everyone expects actually performs worse than polyurethane under sustained static load
3. **Shoe support fails after 6 hours** — if your shift is 8-12 hours, you've been walking on nothing for 2-6 of them
4. **Nursing is physically more dangerous than construction** — almost no one knows this, and the shoes people wear reflect that ignorance
5. **Foam clogs are NOT recommended by podiatrists** — despite widespread use among nurses, Dr. Smik specifically warns against them for lacking heel counter and arch support
