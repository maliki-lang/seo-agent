## Competitor Gap Blueprint

### CRITICAL GAPS (found in every competitor)
- **Standing ≠ walking**: Every article conflates them. Standing causes static compression of the plantar fascia and reduced blood flow. Walking distributes load dynamically. Most "comfortable shoes" are designed for walking. This is the thesis no competitor articulates.
- **No Singapore occupational context**: Hospital floors (NUH, SGH), school corridors (MOE schools), mall marble (Orchard, VivoCity), hawker centres (wet floors). These are specific standing environments with specific requirements — none addressed.
- **Memory foam failure for standing**: Memory foam compresses under sustained static load and doesn't recover. For walkers this is bad; for people who stand still it's even worse (no step-recovery cycle). Links naturally to Sunnystep's existing published article.
- **Foot swelling context**: Feet swell significantly during static standing (gravity + reduced circulation). Wide toe box matters more for standing than walking.

### DIFFERENTIATION OPPORTUNITIES
- Singapore-specific professions: nurses, teachers, retail, F&B, cabin crew
- The standing vs. walking distinction — a genuine educational angle
- Acupressure/circulation tie-in: Sunnystep insole actively stimulates blood flow, which directly addresses the static standing problem

### DATA POINTS TO USE
- Feet built to move — static standing reduces blood flow and keeps muscles contracted (Kadin Foot & Ankle)
- Plantar fasciitis, heel spurs, bunions, Achilles strain = the standing worker injury chain
- Shoe replacement: every 300–500 miles or 6–12 months for daily standing use
