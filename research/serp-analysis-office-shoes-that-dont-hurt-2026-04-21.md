## Competitor Gap Blueprint

### MUST-FILL GAPS (found in 3+ competitors)
- **No biomechanical explanation**: Everyone says "get arch support" but none explains the physics of WHY (weight distribution, pronation cycle, plantar fascia stress). The PSC podiatrist article has this but isn't a shoe brand article.
- **No thesis**: All articles are purely informational. None takes a stance like "the most popular office shoe styles are the worst for your feet."
- **No Singapore walking context**: Long MRT commutes, hard marble/tile floors in offices and malls, 30°C+ heat — none connects these specifically to shoe requirements.

### DIFFERENTIATION OPPORTUNITIES
- **The "comfortable in-store" trap**: Article can argue that most shoes are designed to feel comfortable during a 5-minute trial, not an 8-hour workday — and explain how to test properly.
- **The break-in myth**: "Good shoes don't need breaking in" — none of the competitors say this clearly.
- **The 70–80% stat**: High heels shift 70–80% of body weight to forefoot (podiatrist source). Very shareable, not used by any shoe brand article in SG.
- **Ballet flat myth**: Positioned as "comfortable alternative to heels" but have zero arch support — this is the counterintuitive angle competitors miss.

### DATA TO INCLUDE
- High heels shift 70–80% of body weight to forefoot (PSC podiatrist)
- Ballet flats: "no arch support whatsoever" (Dr. Hillary Brenner via Tread Labs)
- 2–4 cm heel height is the podiatrist-recommended range (PSC)
- Replace shoes every 6–12 months for daily wear

### SUNNYSTEP ANGLE
- Balance Penny Loafer: looks like a classic office shoe, performs like something a physiotherapist would design
- Balance Flex Ballerina: the comfortable alternative to ballet flats that actually has structure
- MIT Sports Lab insole: the biomechanics credibility no competitor can match
