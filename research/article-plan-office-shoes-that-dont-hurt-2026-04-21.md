### 2. The heel problem: what actually happens when you wear heels to the office
- **Type**: body-explanation
- **Word Target**: 300
- **Thesis**: Heels are not a comfort vs. style trade-off — they are structurally designed to shift body weight in a way that causes pain
- **Key data**: High heels shift 70–80% of body weight to forefoot (Singapore podiatrist source). Heel height above 2 cm increases injury risk significantly.
- **Singapore context**: Many Singaporean office dress codes still expect heels — acknowledge this, then offer the solution (kitten heels as minimum viable heel, or structured loafers as full alternative)
- **Voice**: "At Sunnystep, we hear this constantly from customers..."

### 3. The ballet flat trap: why the "comfortable alternative" isn't
- **Type**: body-explanation  
- **Word Target**: 250
- **Thesis**: Ballet flats are marketed as the comfortable alternative to heels. They have zero arch support. This is the most common misconception in office footwear.
- **Key claim**: "Ballet flats provide no arch support whatsoever" (Dr. Hillary Brenner)
- **Singapore floor context**: Hard tiled MRT platforms and office floors — flat shoes transmit every step's impact directly through your foot
- **Pivot**: The comfortable flat exists — it just needs structure. That's a different product from a fashion ballet flat.

### 4. What office shoes that don't hurt actually need
- **Type**: body-how-to
- **Word Target**: 350
- **Structure**: 4 non-negotiable features with plain-English explanations
  1. Heel height 2–4 cm (not flat, not high — the podiatrist sweet spot)
  2. Arch support that holds its shape (polyurethane, not memory foam)
  3. Wide enough toe box (your foot widens under load — it needs room)
  4. Firm heel counter (prevents lateral roll, keeps you aligned)
- **Sunnystep product tie-in**: Acupressure insole, Resilient Polyurethane Core, wide toe box
- **Internal link**: The Science page

### 5. The in-store test most people skip
- **Type**: body-how-to
- **Word Target**: 200
- **Thesis**: Most shoes are designed to feel comfortable during a 5-minute showroom trial. The test for all-day comfort is different.
- **Practical checklist**:
  - Try shoes in the afternoon/evening (feet are largest)
  - Press the heel counter — it should resist, not collapse
  - Walk on hard flooring, not carpet
  - The shoe should feel comfortable immediately, not "after breaking in"
- **Breaking in myth**: If it hurts in the store, it will hurt at the office. Good shoes don't need weeks of adjustment.
